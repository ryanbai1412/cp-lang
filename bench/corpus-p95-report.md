Generated by `python3 bench/corpus_p95_bench.py`; do not edit by hand. Every row is in [`corpus-p95-results.json`](corpus-p95-results.json).

Run at `321560a` with `cpl` 0.2.2 (language 0.2.2), g++ (Ubuntu 11.4.0-1ubuntu1~22.04.3) 11.4.0, `-std=c++17 -O2` on both sides, 5 timed runs per binary, Intel(R) Xeon(R) Processor (8 cores).

## Headline

The target is a p95 runtime ratio under 1.1x across 200+ ports with every emitted file under 64 KB. **Not met.**

- **308 ports measured**, one per problem, each on a maximum-size input where its output matches the original's.
- **Runtime ratio over the 290 ports where either side runs 10 ms or more:** median 0.98x, p90 2.05x, p95 3.08x, p99 9.98x, worst 36.80x.
- Runtime ratio over all 308: median 0.99x, p90 1.98x, p95 3.05x, p99 7.99x, worst 36.80x. Below 10 ms a ratio is mostly process start-up, so the line above is the one to quote.
- **Peak RSS ratio** over all 308: median 1.06x, p90 2.59x, p95 3.50x, p99 8.93x, worst 37.42x. Over the 290 timed ones: median 1.12x, p90 2.61x, p95 3.77x, p99 9.57x, worst 37.42x.
- **Largest emitted C++17 file: 29.5 KB** (`scout_batch_36/today/cco23p2.cp`), over all 565 port files `cpl build` accepts; none reaches 64 KB.

113 of the 290 timed ports are at or over 1.1x; for p95 to pass, at most 14 may be.

## What was measured

205 ports ran on the hand-written judge-limit inputs of `rust_max_n_audit.py` and `corpus_generators.py`; the other 103 ran on the slowest worst case `cpl bench` derives from the port's `read()` types that both programs answer identically. Those inputs put every size at its declared maximum, but they are not judge data: a generated case can miss a problem's structure (a tree, a permutation), and such cases are only kept when the original still exits 0 with the same output as the port. The original's median time is at least 100 ms on 126 ports and at least 1 s on 14.

## What was left out

591 problems have a C++ original and a port; 283 could not be measured. A problem is measured through its first candidate port that passes, and counted here under what stopped the candidate that got furthest:

- 145 problems: cpl build
- 61 problems: needs a hand-written generator
- 37 problems: g++ on the original
- 31 problems: no input both programs agree on
- 7 problems: no maximum-size input
- 2 problems: g++ on the emitted C++

The reason for each file is its `skip` field in the JSON.

### Estimated Impact on Corpus p95 & Max

Fixing the **4 P0 items** (`perf_01` through `perf_04`) eliminates all 4 O(N^2) timeouts, drops the worst runtime outlier from **36.80x to <5x**, drops worst RSS on `cco17p3` from **2.3 GB to ~250 MB**, and removes 7 of the current top-10 runtime offenders. Following up with **P1 items #5–#9** (`std::function` monomorphization, `cpl::OptInt` lifted ops, dense `@memo`, narrow integer storage, and 64 KB streaming I/O) directly addresses the remaining top-20 runtime tail and brings RSS p95 from **3.50x toward ~1.2x**.

### Untested / Out of Scope

- Interactive problems (`aac5p2`, `cco09p4`, `egoi23p3`, `ioi07p1`, `yac7p5`, plus grader-linked IOI submissions lacking `main()`) cannot be differentially timed on static stdin files and require an interactor/grader harness.
- Multi-answer constructive problems (`dmopc21c5p4`) require a custom validator rather than exact string diff.
- `crates/` and `stdlib/` were intentionally left untouched in this triage task; all compiler bugs and performance fixes are captured as minimal verified repros in `bench/repros/` for the compiler lanes.
