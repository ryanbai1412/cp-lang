## What was measured

40 ports ran on the hand-written judge-limit inputs of `rust_max_n_audit.py`; the other 54 ran on the slowest worst case `cpl bench` derives from the port's `read()` types that both programs answer identically. Those inputs put every size at its declared maximum, but they are not judge data: a generated case can miss a problem's structure (a tree, a permutation), and such cases are only kept when the original still exits 0 with the same output as the port. The original's median time is at least 100 ms on 37 ports and at least 1 s on 5.

## What was left out

576 problems have a C++ original and a port; 482 could not be measured. A problem is measured through its first candidate port that passes, and counted here under what stopped the candidate that got furthest:

- 202 problems: cpl build
- 124 problems: needs a hand-written generator
- 94 problems: no maximum-size input
- 28 problems: no input both programs agree on
- 25 problems: g++ on the original
- 9 problems: g++ on the emitted C++

374 problems have a port `cpl build` accepts, so 200 measured ports is within reach of inputs alone. A port under `needs a hand-written generator` reads inside a loop or function, where `cpl bench` has no types to derive a maximum-size input from: give it a generator in `SPECS` in `rust_max_n_audit.py` and the next sweep measures it.

The reason for each file is its `skip` field in the JSON.
