## 1. understand (07:04–07:13, 9 min)
CPL is a Python-indented language for competitive programming that compiles each solution to one readable, self-contained C++17 file you submit to any judge. Over C++ it makes the statement's constraints into types (`let n: 1..=2e5 = read()` reads, validates and picks the width, so no `long long` to forget), replaces sentinels with `?Int` optionals and typed index domains (`domain V = 1..=n`, 1-based arrays without `-1` juggling), and folds the contest library (segment tree, DSU, Dijkstra, Mod) into one-liners. Its third selling point is debugging: a brute (`@alt`) and generator live in the same file, `cpl stress` finds and shrinks a failing case, and a time-travel trace shows where the two runs diverge.
Read: home, /learn/start, /learn/from/cpp, /docs (getting started, cheat sheet, tour). Doubts: the site says "The 0.3.0 language is specified but not yet implemented" while the docs/tools are 0.2.2 and examples are a mix of `None` and `none`, `@memo` vs `@cache`; badges (runs/checks/0.3 spec) are needed to know what will actually compile.
