## The statement's joint bound `a b <= 1e18` can't be written
- **Where:** `let (a, b): (1..=10 ** 18, 1..=10 ** 18) = read()`, then `a * b + 1` and `(a * b) % 4`.
- **What felt wrong:** the statement bounds the product, not just each side. The read type can only say each is at most 1e18, so `a * b` can overflow as far as the types know, and the overflow check (or the reader) has to take the statement's word that it fits. The original C++ multiplies `int64_t`s on the same promise.
- **Goal it hurts:** hard-to-get-wrong, evidence (the type no longer carries the proof the arithmetic needs).
- **Ideal code:** `let (a, b): (1..=10 ** 18, 1..=10 ** 18) where a * b <= 10 ** 18 = read()`, with the checked read trapping on a bad input and the product typed `1..=10 ** 18`.
- **General fix:** a read-time constraint across fields (`where` on a tuple read), checked once at input, that range analysis can use. Ports with "sum of n over tests" or "n · m ≤ 2e5" bounds need the same thing.
- **Severity:** low (the inputs honour it).
- **Owner:** compiler.
