## "Largest free value <= x" is a DSU label instead of a sorted set
- **Where:** port lines 19-24, `var free = DsuOf<V, Int>(init: v => v.ord, join: min)` … `let v = free.get(V(x))` / `if v > 0: free.unite(V(v - 1), V(x))`, with `domain V = 0..=n` so that position 0 can mean "none left".
- **Dream:** `var free = SortedSet<V>.of([v for v in V])` then `if let v = free.range(..=V(x)).last: free.remove(v)` over `domain V = 1..=n`.
- **What felt wrong:** the DSU form needs a sentinel position (0) and an extra domain slot, and the reader has to know the "label points at the free value below" trick. The dream says the greedy directly.
- **Goal it hurts:** performance (the dream is 6.8x slower than the port at max: 0.171 s / 23.7 MB against 0.025 s / 13.7 MB) and hard-to-get-wrong (the port's `0 = none` sentinel).
- **Ideal code:** the dream's two lines, at DSU speed.
- **General fix:** a std "free slots" structure over a domain (take the largest or smallest free position at or below or above x, then mark it used) backed by the DSU trick, returning `?V`. Greedy-matching ports (assign each item the best still-free slot) hit the same pattern. `SortedSet` stays the general ordered set; `Bitset.prev` is the right shape but is \(O(K / 64)\) per call, which an adversarial "all low values taken" input makes quadratic.
- **Severity:** medium.
- **Owner:** stdlib.
