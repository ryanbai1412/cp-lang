A loop that keeps `lo` and `hi`, probes two thirds `lo + (hi - lo) / 3` and `hi - (hi - lo) / 3`, and drops the worse third is a ternary search for the extremum of a unimodal function. Written by hand it needs a stopping rule (`hi - lo > 2`) and a final scan of what is left, and either is easy to get wrong by one. `ternary_min(lo..hi, f)!` and `ternary_max(lo..hi, f)!` (`std.search`) return the position of the extremum over the range by golden-section search, which reuses one probe per step: about \(\log_\varphi n + 3\) calls of `f`, fewer than the hand loop's \(2 \log_{3/2} n\). A loop that divides by 3 once per step, such as counting base-3 digits, is not reported. There is no automatic fix, because whether the loop wants the min or the max, and what it does with the range afterwards, has to be read.
