`floor(sqrt(n))` is C++'s `(int)sqrt(n)`: it rounds `n` to an `F64`, takes a float root and rounds down. Past 2**52 the float root can land just under a whole number, so the result is one too small for some perfect squares (and one too big just below them). `n.isqrt` (`std.math`) is the exact integer square root, the largest `r` with `r * r <= n`, for every `Int`. The fix applies when the operand is a name or field whose type is `Int` or an integer range, written `floor` or `trunc` of `sqrt(n)`, `sqrt(F64(n))` or `n.sqrt`.
