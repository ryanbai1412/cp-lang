Compressing the values, then walking left to right and adding "items so far minus those not above this one" from a Fenwick tree of counts, is the textbook inversion count. Written by hand it needs the compression, the tree, the right cut (`<=` or `<` decides whether ties count) and the position arithmetic. `inversions(xs)` from `std.seq` counts the pairs `i < j` with `xs[i] > xs[j]` (ties are not inversions) in \(O(n \log n)\) on any sequence, with no compression. The fix replaces the tree, the counter and the loop with `let inv = a.inversions()`; `std.seq` declares it with `@lint` on `inversions`. A Fenwick loop that keeps per-item counts rather than summing an inversion count is not reported.
