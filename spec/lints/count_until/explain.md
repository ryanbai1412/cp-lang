A loop that steps a value (`p *= 3` or `p += c`) and counts the steps until a condition fails is a search for the smallest step count `k` where the condition fails. The value after `k` steps has a closed form (\(p_0 \cdot c^k\) or \(p_0 + c k\)), so `(0..).find(k => 3 ** k >= n)!` says what is wanted, the smallest `k` with \(3^k \ge n\), in one line and without two counters to keep in step. If the loop's value is still needed afterwards, it is the closed form at that `k`. A step that is not a plain multiply or add, such as `x = (x + 1) / 2`, has no closed form and is not reported. There is no automatic fix, because the counter's other uses have to be read.
