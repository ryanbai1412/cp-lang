A deque of positions whose values only increase, popped at the back while the newest value is no larger and advanced at the front once its position falls out of the window, gives each window's minimum in \(O(1)\) amortized time. Written by hand it is a list, a head index, two comparisons that must agree on `>=` or `>`, and an off-by-one in `i - k`. `Window(min)` from `std.window` keeps that deque for you: `w.push(x)` when an item enters, `w.pop()` when the oldest leaves, and `w.all!` is the minimum of what is in it. `Window(max)` gives the maximum, and any other fold works too (`Window(+)` keeps a running sum). There is no automatic fix, since the window's bounds move with the loop. A monotone stack with no window (the previous smaller element) is not reported.
