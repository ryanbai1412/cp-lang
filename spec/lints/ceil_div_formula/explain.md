`(a + b - 1) / b` is the C++ way to round a quotient up. It names the divisor twice, can overflow where the answer does not, and is right only for `b > 0`. `ceil_div(a, b)` says what it computes, never overflows, and is right for every sign. The fix applies when `b` is a positive literal or a name whose range type starts at 1 or more, where both spellings agree for every `a` (integer `/` rounds down for a positive divisor), and when nothing in `a` or `b` calls anything.
