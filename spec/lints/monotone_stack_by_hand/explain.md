A stack of positions popped while `a[st.last!] < a[i]` is the monotone stack: each popped position's answer is `i`, its nearest later position with a greater value. Written by hand it needs the right comparison (strict or not), the right moment to record the answer, and a sentinel for positions that never get one. `a.next_smaller(by: x => -x)` (`std.seq`) is that walk, \(O(n)\), returning a `[D]?D` with `None` for no answer; `>` is `a.next_smaller()`, `<=` and `>=` add `strict: false`, and `prev_smaller` gives the nearest earlier position. A stack of values, not positions, such as keeping an increasing subsequence, is not reported. There is no automatic fix, because the loop's answers are read in its own form (a `-1` sentinel, an index shift), and that code has to change with them.
