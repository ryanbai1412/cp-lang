There are n towns (1 ≤ n ≤ 2·10⁵) and m possible cables (0 ≤ m ≤ 2·10⁵). Cable i joins towns uᵢ and vᵢ and costs wᵢ (1 ≤ wᵢ ≤ 10⁹). Choose cables so every town is connected to every other, at the least total cost. Print the cost, or −1 if the towns can't all be connected.

**Input:** n and m, then m lines `u v w`. **Output:** one integer.

| Input | Output |
|---|---|
| `4 5`<br/>`1 2 3`<br/>`2 3 1`<br/>`3 4 4`<br/>`1 4 2`<br/>`1 3 5` | `6` |

<Judge id="cheapest-network">

- Kruskal's algorithm: take cables from cheapest up, and keep one whenever it joins two parts not yet connected.
- `es.sorted(by: e => e.w)` sorts the cable records by cost, and `d.unite(e.u, e.v)` on a `Dsu<V>` returns `true` exactly when the cable joins two parts; see [`Dsu`](/docs/ref/std/dsu).
- All towns are connected when n − 1 cables were kept. The cost can reach about 2·10¹⁴, which fits in an `Int`.

</Judge>
