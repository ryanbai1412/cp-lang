There are n stones in a row (1 ≤ n ≤ 2·10⁵) with heights h₁…hₙ (1 ≤ hᵢ ≤ 10⁴). A frog starts on stone 1 and, from stone i, jumps to stone i + 1 or i + 2, paying the absolute difference of the two heights. Print the least total cost to reach stone n.

**Input:** n, then the n heights. **Output:** one number.

| Input | Output |
|---|---|
| `4`<br/>`10 30 40 20` | `30` |

<Judge id="stepping-stones">

- `cost[i]` is the cheapest way to reach stone i; it starts from the one-stone jump and `min=` tries the two-stone jump.
- Greedy (always take the cheaper next jump) fails on `30 10 60 10 60 50`: the DP looks at both ways into each stone.
- With n = 1 the loop `1..n` is empty and the answer is 0.
- [Dynamic programming](/learn/read/dynamic-programming) has this pattern and when greedy fails.

</Judge>
